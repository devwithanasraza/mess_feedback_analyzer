📐 Statistical Modeling & Weighted Satisfaction

Simple arithmetic averages fail to capture dining satisfaction because food taste and hygiene affect student well-being much more than portion size. We implement a multi-attribute weighted linear combination model:

Weighted Satisfaction Score (%) =
[ (Overall Rating × 40%) +
  (Average Taste × 20%) +
  (Average Quality × 15%) +
  (Mess Hygiene × 10%) +
  (Food Freshness × 10%) +
  (Serving Quantity × 5%) ] ÷ 5.0 × 100

Rationale of Weight Assignments:

  • Overall Rating (40%): Captures the aggregate subjective meal satisfaction.
  • Taste (20%) & Quality (15%): Culinary execution and ingredient purity are primary quality indicators.
  • Hygiene (10%) & Freshness (10%): Food safety parameters that maintain health standards in hostel halls.
  • Quantity (5%): Baseline sufficiency check; students can usually take second servings if available.

💬 Transparent Sentiment Classification

To maintain transparency without introducing heavy external neural network dependencies, we implemented a Lexicon-Based Keyword Sentiment Classifier in PHP.

Polarity Score = (Positive Matches - Negative Matches) / (Total Matches + 1)
Classification Thresholds:
• Positive: Score > 0.0 (or Positive Keyword Count > Negative Keyword Count)
• Negative: Score < 0.0 (or Negative Keyword Count > Positive Keyword Count)
• Neutral: Score = 0.0 (Equal or zero keyword matches)

Dictionary Lexicon:

  • Positive Lexicon: good, great, tasty, fresh, excellent, delicious, nice, amazing, superb, crispy, soft, flavorful, satisfying, perfect, clean, hygienic, love, best, well
  • Negative Lexicon: bad, poor, cold, oily, salty, bland, tasteless, dirty, unhealthy, waste, sour, hard, stale, smelly, delayed, watery, undercooked, burnt, insects, hair, horrible

🍛 Dish Performance Score (DPS) Formula

Mess managers need a single comparative index for each recipe to determine whether a dish should be retained, modified, or replaced.

Dish Performance Score =
  Average Taste + Average Quality + Average Freshness
  + Repeat Preference Bonus (+1.0 / +0.5 / -0.5)
  - Plate Wastage Penalty (0.0 to 2.5)

Performance Tiers:

  • Excellent Score ≥ 12.0: Highly popular, low waste dish.
  • Good Score 10.0 - 11.9: Reliable staple meal item.
  • Average Score 8.0 - 9.9: Minor recipe or temperature improvements needed.
  • Needs Improvement Score < 8.0: High waste or consistently low ratings.

🗄️ Relational Database Design: Why MySQL?

"Why MySQL instead of NoSQL or CSV storage?"
College mess operations require structured relational integrity. A single meal is linked to multiple dishes, which in turn receive hundreds of feedback ratings and wastage incident records over time.

MySQL provides:

  • Foreign Key Cascades: Ensuring orphan records are avoided if menus change.
  • High-Performance Aggregations: COUNT(), AVG(), SUM(), and GROUP BY executed in milliseconds with B-Tree indexes on menu_date and meal_type.
  • ACID Transactions: Multi-table consistency when recording overall feedback, dish items, wastage, and sentiment simultaneously.

🛡️ Ethical Data Science & Student Privacy

Students often fear reprisal or hostility from mess contractors if their honest critical feedback is tied to their identity. This project implements Privacy by Design:

  • No personal identifying information (PII) such as Name, Roll Number, Phone, or Email is requested or stored.
  • No IP addresses are logged in the database tables.
  • Spam and rapid duplicate submissions are regulated using non-identifying cryptographic session hashes.
  • CSV exports strictly contain aggregated dining evaluation numbers and comments only.

🎓 1st-Year B.Tech Viva Q&A Quick Reference

Q1: What problem does this system solve?

Ans: It provides anonymous feedback collection for students to review meals without fear, while giving mess managers data-backed insights into dish ratings, plate waste root causes, and recipe improvements.

Q2: How is food wastage correlated with menu planning?

Ans: The system calculates plate wastage levels ('None', 'Very Little', 'Some', 'A lot') against specific dishes, meal sessions, and portion sizes ('Too Little', 'Appropriate', 'Too Much'). If 80% of waste occurs when portion is marked 'Too Much', standard ladle sizes are adjusted.

Q3: How does the sentiment classification work?

Ans: It tokenizes student text comments and matches words against positive and negative dictionaries, computing a polarity score to classify sentiment into Positive, Neutral, or Negative.

Q4: What is the future scope?

Ans: Future extensions include computer vision-based plate-waste estimation at tray disposal counters, machine learning forecasting for daily meal turnout, and mobile app push notifications for daily specials.
